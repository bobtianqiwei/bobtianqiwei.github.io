dear___

A mobile memory album that lets people approach emotionally charged photographs slowly, partially, and temporarily.

In April 2026, I learned I was moving to Boston for MIT. My partner and I decided to part ways. The distance and the years ahead were too much to ask of each other.

In the months that followed, I found myself living inside what Hiroshi Ishii calls TeleAbsence: picking up objects my partner had touched, looking at old photographs, feeling a presence slowly becoming absence.

So I built something — a private photo album of 48 photographs from our years together, with every person masked under a white cover generated by a computer vision model, alongside 48 short texts addressed to my partner across time, and music they had once sent me. Opening the album, I'd see only ghosted figures in familiar places. Erasing a mask brought the figure back, the text surfaced, the music began. Then, after a pause, the mask slowly returned, and presence became absence again.

I hadn't set out to build an app. I was trying to hold something I didn't yet have words for, talking to someone I loved across time, and through that, to an earlier version of myself.

I showed it to a close friend once. She watched the figures fade from the photographs, and she cried. So did I.

That reaction told me the interaction itself — the erasure, the slow return of absence, the layering of image, text, and music, might mean something beyond my own archive. dear___ grew from that instinct.

The Idea

Personal photo archives make memories immediately visible. But after loss or separation, seeing a photograph can bring past closeness back all at once, intensified by the pain of present absence.

dear___ explores another way of encountering personal memories. Instead of permanently displaying or deleting a photograph, users can cover people or regions with editable white masks, reveal them through touch, and watch the mask gradually return.

The Interaction

Approach memories slowly

Begin with a covered photograph. The memory remains present without revealing everything at once.

Reveal through touch

Erase the cover with your finger. The photograph, diary, and music return at your own pace.

Layer photos, words, and music

Compose each memory with an image, diary text, dates, and music that belong together.

Let absence return

After a pause, the cover gradually restores itself. Presence becomes temporary again.

Choose what to share

Decide whether to include the cover, diary, and time information before saving or sharing.

Usage Scenario

Eli is navigating the end of a close relationship. He does not want to delete the photographs, songs, and messages that remain, but seeing them fully in an ordinary gallery makes the happiness of the past return all at once, sharpened by the pain of present absence.

He creates an album in dear___, imports a small group of photographs, and places white masks over the person who is no longer present. He adjusts each mask by hand, writes short notes that read like diary entries or unsent letters, and attaches songs that still carry the shape of that time.

Later, Eli opens the album. Familiar places appear first—a doorway, a table, a corner of a room—while the person remains covered as a white silhouette. He slowly erases part of the mask. As the figure emerges, the diary text becomes visible and the music begins. After a short pause, the mask returns, and the memory settles back into absence.

The interaction does not restore the person or erase them. It gives Eli a controlled way to approach their traces, sit with what remains, and address them across time rather than all at once. Over repeated visits, composing masks, writing unsent letters, and watching covers return become small reflective rituals.

Technical Implementation

Architecture and interaction
dear___ is a native SwiftUI application for iOS 17. A main-actor store manages Codable album data and local media. Vision and Core Image generate editable person covers off the main thread, while a custom Canvas handles painting, erasing, touch reveal, gradual restoration, and synchronized audio through AVFoundation and MusicKit.

Storage and export
Atomic local saves, optional iCloud synchronization, and ZIP backups protect the archive. Backups can merge by content hash to avoid duplicates. The app also supports date-based memory notifications, configurable share cards, and exhibition packages that include only the layers and consent settings selected by the user.

dear___: Ephemeral Reveal for Interactive Absence in Personal Photo Archives

Bob Tianqi Wei and Hiroshi Ishii
UIST 2026 demo
SUBMITTED

ABSTRACT

Personal photo archives preserve traces of people, places, and moments, but they often make memory access fast, flat, and fully visible. For emotionally charged memories, immediate visibility may not always be the desired mode of interaction. We present dear___, a mobile memory album that explores ephemeral reveal as an interaction pattern for TeleAbsence. In dear___, users compose personal photos with editable white masks, diary text, music, and temporal metadata. A user can erase the mask through touch to reveal the photograph and its associated layers; after a short interval, the mask gradually returns, making the encounter partial, temporary, and reversible. The system also supports computer-vision-assisted mask generation, manual cover editing, memory notifications, share cards, and consent-aware export. Rather than simulating an absent person, dear___ works with existing traces and lets users decide how presence should briefly appear, fade, and return to absence. This demo contributes a working mobile system and interaction concept for designing absence as an interactive material in personal photo archives.
